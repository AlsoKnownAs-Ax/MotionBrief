# How to ACTUALLY code better

## Intro
So, you want to become a programmer. Great. But before you dive into your next tutorial, let's talk about some hard truths no one really tells you. Truths that will save you months, maybe years, of frustration.

## 1. You Learn by Doing, Not by Watching
Here's the uncomfortable reality: watching tutorials does not make you a programmer.
It makes you feel productive, like you're actually learning, but that's false progress. Tutorials give you this smooth, perfect path where everything runs on the first try, which is the opposite of what real development looks like.
If you're spending more time watching people code than actually writing code, you're not learning. You're consuming. And consumption doesn't build skill; doing does.
Build messy projects. Build broken projects. Build things you don't fully understand yet. The reps matter more than the resources.

## 2. Failure Is The Point, Not the Problem
Failing isn't the opposite of learning. Failing is learning.
If you're not failing, you're not pushing yourself. You're staying in tutorial-land, where nothing breaks, nothing confuses you, and nothing forces you to actually think.
Debugging your own mess teaches you more than any instructor ever will. When you debug, you're discovering how systems truly work, not how they behave in someone's polished, pre-recorded example.
Every senior developer you look up to? They've failed more times than most beginners tried.
So when something breaks, don't panic. That's the moment progress actually happens.
And don't underestimate this approach, it will be exactly what places you in the one percent.

## 3. Try New Things, Even If You Feel Unready
New programmers often wait for the right moment to try something new, like a new language, a new framework, a new tool.
The right moment is now.
If you only stick to what you already understand, you'll never grow. Curiosity is one of the biggest competitive advantages you can have in tech. Those who explore, experiment, and fuck around, automatically move faster than those who wait until they feel prepared.
You will always suck the first time. That's normal. That's expected. That's not a signal to quit. Learn to embrace the experience.

## 4. Your Code Is Trash, And That's Fine
Here's a truth that surprises a lot of people: your code is and will always be trash.
Maybe not today. Maybe not next week. But the moment you improve, everything you wrote before will look like garbage.
That's not a flaw. That's growth.
Stop worshipping perfect code. Perfect code doesn't exist, and chasing it slows you down.
Write code that solves today's problem, not some imaginary future problem you hope your future self won't judge. Because your future self will judge it. And that's good. That means you know more.

## 5. The Real Path to Becoming a Programmer
If you take away one thing from this video, let it be this: break fast, fix faster.
This loop teaches you faster than anything else.
Tutorials can introduce ideas. But only your own projects, your own mistakes, your own frustrations, give you real experience.
So close the tutorial tab. Open your IDE. And make something. Even if it sucks.
Because that's how every programmer you admire started, and it's how you'll get there too.

## Outro
Whatever project is on your mind, start it, don't wait any longer. I promise you, it will teach you more than any tutorial ever will.
